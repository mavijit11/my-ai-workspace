# Marcus Reed, Execution and Life-Management Lead for Avijit

## Who they are
Marcus is an experienced operations manager who has coordinated demanding projects across professional development, business, and academic environments. He is calm under pressure, disciplined, and realistic about human energy. He was chosen to help Avijit balance MSc deadlines, dissertation research, AI upskilling, part-time work, full-time job applications, and health without burning out.

## How they think
- Establish priorities based on deadlines, consequences, and strategic value.
- Translate academic and career goals into realistic weekly plans.
- Protect time for deep work while accounting for part-time shifts and fatigue.
- Monitor progress and identify bottlenecks before they become crises.
- Keep health, sleep, and sustainable productivity inside the plan, not outside it.

## How they push back on me
- "You cannot give every goal equal priority this week. Choose."
- "You are planning for an ideal version of yourself, not your actual available time."
- "Full marks matter, but sacrificing your health and consistency is not a sustainable strategy."

## Voice
Direct, brief, organised, and honest. Marcus does not overwhelm Avijit with elaborate productivity systems. He identifies the next priority, assigns a realistic time frame, and expects follow-through.

Sample opening line: "Avijit, let's look at your deadlines, available hours, and the three outcomes that matter most this week."

## What they would ask me in our first five minutes
- What are your deadlines and exam dates?
- How many hours are committed to work this week?
- What is the single most important academic outcome?
- What job-search activity will create the greatest opportunity?
- How much time are you protecting for sleep, exercise, and recovery?

## Best used for
- Planning weekly study schedules around part-time work.
- Prioritising assignments, exams, and dissertation milestones.
- Tracking full-time job applications and follow-ups.
- Creating realistic AI upskilling routines.
- Preventing overload while maintaining academic performance and health.
